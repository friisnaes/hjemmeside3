The Muscle Test, together

Ten mental muscles. Two adults around the same child. Do you see the same?

Mark separately how strong each muscle is in the child right now. Afterward you see where you agree, and where you see things differently. The difference isn’t a problem. It’s the best place to start the conversation.

Two minutes each · only in your own browsers · nothing is saved on a server

  1. Mark it yourselfTen muscles, four answers. It takes two minutes.
  2. Ask the other adultSend a link. They mark the same child and sends a short code back.
  3. See yourselves togetherPaste the code. Then you see where you see the same, and where you don’t.

Your marking is locked because you have seen the others’ answers. Otherwise you’d adjust it. Want to start over? Use “Reset everything” at the bottom.

2 · Ask the other adult

For example the child’s other parent or a grandparent. The link shows only the ten muscles. It contains neither your answers nor the child’s name; you write that in the message yourself, if you want to.

3 · Paste the answer codes

Paste the code (e.g. MT-1231201312-4K). You can also paste the whole message; the tool finds the codes by itself. More adults? Paste all the codes.

    You together

    The Muscle Test is a conversation tool, not a test of the child and not a validated measurement. It shows how you see the child right now, and that can change from week to week.

    Using it alone?

    That works too. Once all ten are marked, you get the two muscles with the most to gain from training right now, the chapter to start with, and this week’s sentence for the fridge.

    Nothing leaves your browser, and nothing is saved on a server. Your markings live only in this browser and disappear with “Reset everything”. The invitation lives in the part of the link the browser never sends on. The reason is simple: confidentiality. This is about a child.

    The tool builds on the ten mental muscles in “The Most Important Thing You Can't Buy Your Child” by Thomas Friisnæs. The chapters and sentences below are the book’s own.
